# LinkedIn Event Follow-up DM

Use when Nash has already met the person (event, talk, class, mixer) and wants to follow up in LinkedIn chat. For cold first contact through Sales Navigator, use the sales-navigator-inmail skill instead.

Inputs: (1) the person's profile (screenshot, PDF or pasted text), (2) the event and when it was, (3) Nash's purpose (venture interviews, career/PM advice, research, etc.). If Nash pastes his own draft, edit it rather than rewriting from scratch.

## Step 1: Pick one hook and one purpose

- Read the full profile. Pick the single profile fact that connects most directly to Nash's purpose (a current role, a specific system they built, a past pivot). Specific beats flattering.
- State the connection, not just the fact: why does their experience bear on Nash's problem? (e.g. "you've shipped CRM-OMS workflows" -> "getting teams to adopt a new workflow on top of messy systems is what we keep running into").
- One main purpose per thread. If Nash has a second one (e.g. PM career advice), keep it to one clause or save it for the call itself.
- Pull venture context (problem statement, HMW) from project docs or the conversation. Never invent traction, metrics or interview counts.

## Step 2: Write as separate chat messages

LinkedIn DM reads like chat, so send 2-4 short messages instead of one letter. Each message 1-3 sentences.

1. **Greeting + where we met**: name, event, when. Optional one line of warmth ("wish we'd had more time to talk").
2. **Who Nash is + what he's working on**: Berkeley exchange student; the problem in one or two plain sentences. No jargon, no pitch.
3. **Hook + ask**: the profile fact, why it connects, one concrete question only they can answer, and a small time-bounded ask ("a 15 to 20 minute call in the next two weeks?"). Add "happy to work around your schedule".
   - When asking about their experience or network, open that sentence with "I'm wondering if..." (e.g. "I'm wondering if you have experience with this or know someone who does."). Nash finds this more polite than a bare "If you have..." or a direct question.
4. **Sign-off**: "Thanks!\nNash" (can merge into message 3).

## Step 3: Light English pass, keep his voice

Nash writes these himself in a casual tone. Keep the tone; fix only grammar and unclear phrasing. Common fixes:
- "It's great to see you" (about the past) -> "It was great to see you"
- "yesterday night" -> "last night"
- "is pity that I didn't have much talk with you" -> "Pity we didn't get to talk more"
- "I noticed about your experience of" -> "I noticed your experience with"
- "I will like to" -> "I'd love to"
- "If you have free time, I will like to have a talk" -> a specific ask with duration and timeframe
- "If you can have any connection" -> "I'm wondering if you have experience with this or know someone who does"
- Lowercase "i" -> "I"

List each fix in one line so Nash learns the pattern.

## Style rules

- English, warm, human, short. No em dashes. No hype or overclaiming.
- Polite softener: use "I'm wondering if..." for asks about the person's experience or connections.
- Emoji: none by default. Keep at most one only if Nash's own draft has one.
- Total length across messages: aim under ~600 characters.

## Follow-up rule

If no reply after 5-7 days, draft one short nudge (1-2 sentences) that adds something new (a specific question, a relevant finding), not "just bumping this". Stop after one nudge.

## Output format

Reply in Chinese with English terms. Give:
1. The messages, labeled 1/2/3, each ready to paste.
2. Grammar fixes list (if Nash supplied a draft).
3. One line on which profile fact was used as the hook and why.
4. One question Nash should have ready if they say yes.
